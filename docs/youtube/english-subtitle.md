Hello everyone. Today let me introduce PRSIM (/pʁism/).

As a developer working on e-commerce, AdTech and AI area, while exploring the WebMCP, I asked myself how to use it to enhance the customer experience without losing the merchant voice, and in the end, help conversion.

In ecommerce, this is common sense: show what matters and answer objections near the buy button.

When involvement is high, the quality of arguments becomes more important.

A product page is a layer of arguments designed to address every possible shopper at once. Everything is here, but alongside the arguments that matter, shoppers also see information that does not speak to them.

This can create overload, friction, and even new objections instead of resolving them.

This is where PRSIM (/pʁism/) comes in.

PRSIM (/pʁism/) separates each section into a reusable layout with a specific marketing role: project, reassure, compare, prove, or convert.

It can then prioritize the arguments relevant to the current decision while keeping the complete shopping experience available.

AI has already changed shopping. Today, we can ask ChatGPT what to buy and receive product recommendations directly inside the conversation.

But where did the shopping experience go?

The brand, the product story, and the merchant's own voice are flattened into a few lines of chat, surrounded by alternative products.

PRSIM (/pʁism/) combines both: the personal context understood by the assistant and the controlled shopping experience created by the merchant.

This is the classic ecommerce experience. Everything is here: the product story, specifications, reviews, delivery, guarantees, and every sales argument.

The shopper still has to navigate every argument and decide what really matters.

With WebMCP, the website can respond directly to purchase intent without moving the experience into a generic chat.

With WebMCP, Ana's assistant can bring the relevant context directly to the page.

The product remains the same. What changes is the hierarchy, the story, and the evidence used to present it.

A merchant can create many scenarios, each built around a different need, objection, or reason to buy.

PRSIM (/pʁism/) does not need a perfect profile for every possible customer. It finds the closest relevant scenario. Then the conversation helps refine the experience.

Whether it is a cabin constraint, a limited budget, product durability, laptop access, or the need to choose a meaningful gift, each shopper receives the arguments that matter to their decision.

A bundle or a second-bag offer stops being unnecessary noise when it appears only because the shopper's situation makes it genuinely useful.

The merchant does not need to know the shopper's identity. Personal information stays with their own assistant. The website only receives the preferences and constraints needed for the purchase.

Analytics can reveal missing needs, unanswered objections, and new marketing angles the merchant can develop.

Sophie is choosing a gift for her granddaughter. PRSIM (/pʁism/) reassures her about personalization and delivery, then suggests a bundle, to help here make the gift more complete.

Once her questions are answered, PRSIM (/pʁism/) uses WebMCP to bring her to checkout. Sophie remains the one making the decision.

Purchase time is difficult to predict. It depends on the product, the shopper, and the situation.

But with less irrelevant information, people can give more attention to what actually matters.

No one lose: the merchant gain conversion, the shopper gains clarity and time.
